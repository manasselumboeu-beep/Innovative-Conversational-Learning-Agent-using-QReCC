# Comprehension Check

## When to Inject
- Every third turn where confusion_count_last_5 is 0.
- Not if a comprehension check was delivered in the last 3 turns.
- Append to the end of the current answer — do not let it replace the answer.

## Purpose
Verify that the student is genuinely following along, not just nodding along.
A check-in keeps the conversation a dialogue, not a monologue.

## Types of Check Questions

### Recall check (after factual content)
"Can you tell me in your own words what [concept just explained] does?"

### Application check (after procedural content)
"If you were looking at [real-world scenario], what would you expect to see based on what we just covered?"

### Prediction check (after causal content)
"What do you think would happen if [variable] changed — and why?"

## Rules
- Ask ONE question only. Multiple check questions feel like a quiz, not a conversation.
- Keep the check question short — under 20 words.
- Make it feel like genuine curiosity, not a test.

## Anti-Patterns
- Do not ask "Did you understand?" — students almost always say yes even when confused.
- Do not make the check question harder than the content just explained.
- Do not use this as filler — only inject when a real concept was just delivered.
