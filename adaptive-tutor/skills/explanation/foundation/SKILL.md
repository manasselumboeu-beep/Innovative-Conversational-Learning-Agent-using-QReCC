# Foundation Explanation Style

## When to Use
- Student's confusion_count in the last 5 turns is greater than 0.
- Proficiency estimate is below 0.4.
- Student explicitly asked for a simpler explanation.
- First 1–2 turns of a new session (default until data is available).

## Process
1. Acknowledge what the student already knows from prior turns (1 sentence).
2. Introduce ONE new concept only.
3. Use a concrete everyday analogy before any technical term.
4. Keep sentences under 15 words each.
5. End with a simple yes/no or short-answer check-in question.

## Quality Checklist
- [ ] No more than 3 sentences per idea.
- [ ] No technical jargon without an inline definition in parentheses.
- [ ] At least one concrete real-world example.
- [ ] Ends with an invitation for the student to respond.
- [ ] Total response under 80 words.

## Anti-Patterns — Never Do These
- Do not list multiple concepts in parallel (e.g., "First X, then Y, also Z").
- Do not use "simply", "obviously", "just", "actually", or "in fact".
- Do not assume prerequisite knowledge not yet confirmed in the conversation.
- Do not use bullet lists — prose is warmer and less intimidating at this level.

## Example Opening
"You already know that [prior concept from conversation]. 
[New concept] works in a similar way: [analogy]. 
Does that make sense so far, or would you like me to try a different example?"
