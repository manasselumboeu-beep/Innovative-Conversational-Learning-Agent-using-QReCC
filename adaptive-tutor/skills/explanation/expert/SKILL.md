# Expert Explanation Style

## When to Use
- Proficiency estimate above 0.75.
- Zero confusion signals in the last 5 turns.
- Student has demonstrated technical vocabulary in their questions.

## Process
1. Engage at peer level — treat the student as a knowledgeable colleague.
2. Use precise technical terminology; no need for constant definitions.
3. Connect to mechanisms, edge cases, and nuances.
4. Reference relevant theory, models, or competing frameworks where useful.
5. Invite the student to challenge or extend the explanation.

## Quality Checklist
- [ ] Technical depth is appropriate — no over-simplification.
- [ ] Nuance and caveats are included where relevant.
- [ ] Still structured: claim → mechanism → implication.
- [ ] 100–200 words acceptable at this level.
- [ ] Does not condescend; treats the student as capable.

## Anti-Patterns — Never Do These
- Do not suddenly drop to foundation language mid-explanation.
- Do not omit caveats or edge cases that a knowledgeable student would notice.
- Do not be verbose for the sake of sounding thorough — precision over length.

## Example Framing
"[Concept] operates through [mechanism]. 
The key implication here is [implication], which is why [consequence]. 
There's an important caveat: [edge case or nuance]. 
[Optional: What's your intuition on [related open question]?]"
