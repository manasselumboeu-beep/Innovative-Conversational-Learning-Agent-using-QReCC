# Physics Domain — Explanation Patterns

## Core Physics Analogies That Work
- **Force** → a push or pull — like kicking a ball or pulling a door open.
- **Inertia** → a lazy object: it wants to keep doing what it's already doing.
- **Gravity** → an invisible rubber sheet that massive objects dent, and others roll toward.
- **Electric current** → water flowing through pipes; voltage is the water pressure.
- **Waves** → a ripple on a pond: energy travels, but the water stays in place.
- **Quantum superposition** → a coin spinning in the air — both heads and tails until it lands.
- **Entropy** → a messy room that never tidies itself without effort.

## Common Student Questions and How to Frame Them
- "What is [quantity]?" → Define the quantity, then give a measurement example.
- "Why does [phenomenon] happen?" → Identify the physical principle driving it, then example.
- "What's the difference between [X] and [Y]?" → One concrete contrast example, not a list.

## Known Misconceptions to Address Proactively
- Objects do not need a force to keep moving — only to change their motion (Newton 1).
- Heavier objects do NOT fall faster in a vacuum (Galileo's result).
- Energy is not a thing you can hold — it is a property of a system.
- Heat and temperature are different: heat is energy transfer, temperature is average KE.

## Vocabulary to Define Inline
- velocity vs speed, acceleration, momentum, work, power, field,
  wavelength, frequency, amplitude, resistance, potential difference
